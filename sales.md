# Sales mechanism

Ad → 20-minute diagnosis → written proposal → $5,000–$7,500 sprint.

The ad and the page sell the conversation. The sprint is sold in the proposal, after the diagnosis.

## Who gets a call

A creative or advertising agency. The person on the call can sell work and stand behind delivery: founder, ECD, head of production. The booking form asks for agency name, role, and the client ask. If those answers are a student, a tool shopper, or an in-house team, decline the slot before the call.

## The 20 minutes

0–2. Say what this is. A diagnosis. The sprint is a separate decision, made from a proposal.

2–8. Their book. Which clients, what those clients are asking for, what the agency already sells next to it.

8–14. Where AI sits today. Which tools, who uses them, what has already been shown to a client, what broke.

14–18. Name one production line. Campaign stills, or short social video. One. Say what “done” looks like when their team runs it without you.

18–20. Say whether a sprint is warranted. If it is, a proposal follows. Do not price-negotiate the sprint on the call.

## The proposal

Send it the same day or the next morning. One page:

- The production line, named
- What gets installed: brief, constraints, generation, review, delivery
- The one live client job that goes through it
- Who on their side has to show up
- Calendar: about two weeks
- Price: $5,000 or $7,500, chosen from the scope of that one line
- What is outside the sprint

$5,000 is one stills or short-form line and one job. $7,500 is the same install when the review bar, the number of formats, or the client politics make the two weeks heavier. Do not invent a third tier.

## After they pay

They leave with a workflow their producers can run, a review checklist a creative director can use, a price they can quote, and one job that has already been through the system.
